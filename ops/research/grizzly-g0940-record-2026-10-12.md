## Exact identity and current configuration

Grizzly identifies the current product as `G0940`, a 13-inch benchtop planer with a 2 HP, 120V single-phase 15A universal motor, a two-row 2-inch helical cutterhead carrying 30 indexable carbide inserts, and a granite table.[1][2] The inserts measure 15 x 15 x 2.5 mm, expose four usable edges, and correspond to Grizzly replacement insert `H9893`.[2][8][12]

The specification sheet reports a 13-inch maximum cut width, 6-inch maximum stock thickness, 25 FPM feed rate, 1/32-inch maximum full-width cut, and 3/32-inch maximum cut on 6-inch-wide stock.[2] These are manufacturer specifications, not independent long-term performance measurements.

## Revision and model boundaries

The downloadable owner manual and illustrated parts list identify V3 coverage for machines manufactured since March 2025.[3][5] The live parts catalog also exposes a separate V4 manual, `P0939MANV4`, for G0939/G0940 machines manufactured October 2025 and later.[4][29] The downloadable V3 manual describes a headstock lock, while the current exact-model FAQ says the G0940 does not include a carriage lock.[3][6] This conflict is treated as revision-sensitive rather than silently harmonized.

The machine manufacture date and serial number must be checked before applying revision-specific instructions or ordering parts. V3 facts must not be transferred automatically to October 2025-and-later machines.

G0939 shares a manual and related chassis but uses three straight HSS knives and a stainless-steel table rather than the G0940 helical insert head and granite table.[16] G0505 is a separate 12-1/2-inch two-knife planer.[17] G0959 is a 12-inch combination planer/jointer with 28 inserts.[18] G0999 is a separate 15-inch two-speed planer with a 32-insert spiral head.[19] Shop Fox W1877 uses a spiral-style head with 26 HSS inserts.[20] G0815 is a separate 15-inch 3 HP heavy-duty planer and is the model in the current CPSC recall.[15]

No specifications, parts claims, owner reports, recall status, or commercial identities are transferred among these products.

## Documentation, parts, and serviceability

The V3 owner manual covers insert rotation/replacement, motor-brush replacement, belt tension and replacement, pinion-gear replacement, lubrication, troubleshooting, cutterhead/table calibration, depth-stop calibration, wiring, and illustrated parts.[3] Grizzly also publishes an exact-model advanced cutterhead-bearing procedure requiring substantial disassembly and an estimated two to four hours.[7] This is meaningful internal repair documentation, but it does not constitute a comprehensive repair manual for every subsystem and production revision. The manual reserves electrical repair for qualified personnel.[3]

Grizzly publishes an exact-model parts catalog and illustrated list with manufacture-date warnings.[4][5] Direct pages identify replacement H9893 inserts, superseded carbon brushes, a superseded Poly-V belt, and the complete helical cutterhead.[9][10][11][12] Current purchase information is mixed for major assemblies such as the motor and rollers, and the parts list expressly says availability is not guaranteed.[4][5] Parts availability is therefore classified as partial, not complete or permanent.

Grizzly states that house-brand products generally carry a one-year warranty for the original purchaser with proof of purchase, subject to published exclusions.[24] Its repair policy provides technical assistance and depot repair intake while documenting freight, authorization, and parts-availability limits.[25]

## Owner evidence and durability limits

A Forestry Forum owner described buying a scratch-and-dent G0940, finding substantial as-received nonparallelism, adjusting it to approximately 0.002 inch, and then reporting a good surface finish while judging the height mechanism and extension tables less robust than those on a DeWalt planer.[13] Because the unit was explicitly scratch-and-dent, its initial condition cannot be generalized to new retail units.

A Sawmill Creek purchaser reported receiving three successive G0940 units damaged during shipping, while also describing Grizzly technical staff as patient and helpful.[14] This supports only a bounded packaging/shipping and support anecdote, not operating reliability or lifespan.

No representative exact-model failure-rate study, repair-rate dataset, multi-year owner cohort, or adequate repair-economics evidence was accepted. Expected service life, population failure rate, annual maintenance cost, street price, and the repair-versus-replace verdict remain T4/unverified.

## Recall boundary

The Grizzly recall page and an exact-product-name CPSC/SaferProducts API query were reviewed. The API returned an empty result for `G0940`.[26][27] The nearby current planer recall applies to G0815, not G0940.[15] These are bounded checks, not proof that G0940 is recall-free or generally safe.
