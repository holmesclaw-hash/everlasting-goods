## Maintenance versus internal repair

The KP0800 owner manual linked from the U.S. KP0800K page documents:

- mini-planer-blade removal, gauge alignment, installation, clearance checks, and paired replacement;
- conventional-blade installation and simultaneous sharpening;
- chip and dust-bag cleaning;
- carbon-brush inspection and paired replacement.

The same manual routes repairs and all undocumented maintenance or adjustment to Makita authorized or factory service. It does not provide motor, bearing, pulley, belt, switch, cord, base, electrical-diagnostic, or internal-reassembly procedures. The parts breakdown identifies components but is not a repair manual. The database therefore records `repair_manual_available = 0` while retaining the owner-manual link and the documented user-serviceable tasks.

## Owner evidence

A ProductReview.com.au exact-name KP0800K report was published on June 10, 2022 and says the planer was bought in July 2020. The owner describes extensive use, mostly on hardwood, no tool problems during that period, and straightforward blade changes. The report also describes nail impacts damaging reversible blades.

This is one anecdotal regional report. It does not establish that the reviewed unit matches the U.S. 120V electrical configuration, controlled usage, maintenance history, failure incidence, or a representative lifespan distribution. It is retained only as bounded owner context; expected service life, failure rate, repair economics, and recommendation remain unverified.
